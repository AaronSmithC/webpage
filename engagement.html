// 01 Who This Is For

The program is written as requirements, not finished designs, because the people who have spent a career on a given thing will design it better. Five kinds of people move it forward.

DOMAIN EXPERTS

Own an Open Item

Geophysics, cryogenic propulsion, structures and fatigue, thermal, radiation transport, human factors. Push any page deep enough and the right answer is a name, not a claim. If a requirement on this site is set at the wrong level, say so. If an open item is yours, take it.

HARDWARE BUILDERS

Partner on the Vehicles

Aegis Station is architect, integrator, and software developer. Structures, tanks, landing gear, pressure vessels, mechanisms, thermal and communications hardware are bought or partnered. Engines and deep-throttling cryogenic propulsion are development partnerships. Each vehicle page carries a make, buy, or partner position you can respond to.

PROGRAM OFFICES & OPERATORS

Team on Near-Term Surface Work

Ice prospecting, regolith mechanics, short-range hoppers, and autonomous water logistics have customers before any station exists. Study contracts, teaming on solicitations, and subcontracts to universities and labs are how the surface program gets built. Bring the requirement you actually have.

INVESTORS

Fund the Next Layer of Evidence

The shop is bootstrapped and software-first. What capital buys next is not a rendering. It is testbed campaigns for the prospecting method, hardware-in-the-loop against the flight software, and subsystem demonstrators with partners. Serious inquiries get the model chain, not a pitch.

POLICY & ACCESS

Shape How It Unfolds

Lunar water at industrial scale is an infrastructure question before it is an engineering one: who builds, who owns, who gets access. The strategy notes on this site are the current position. Disagreement in writing is welcome and gets answered in writing.

// 03 Where It Stops

Honest closure is a rule on every page here, so it applies to the shop itself. These are the limits a collaborator should know before the first call.

Limit 01

Verified in Simulation, Not on Hardware

The flight software has flown complete missions against physics models with known ground truth. It has not flown on a vehicle. Hardware-in-the-loop and testbed campaigns are the next layer, and they need partners with benches.

Limit 02

Hardware Is Built by Partners

There is no machine shop, clean room, or test range behind this site. Every physical article in the reference set is a partnership, and the make, buy, or partner positions on the vehicle pages are proposals, not orders.

Limit 03

One Set of Eyes Until Yours

A single design authority is fast and unchecked in equal measure. The things one person cannot verify are the things a second reader catches first. That is why the pages end in open-items lists instead of conclusions.

Limit 04

Requirements, Not Detailed Design

Each capability is specified to the level that sets the requirement and sizes the vehicle. Detailed design, qualification, and certification are downstream and belong to the people and organizations that do them for a living.

// 04 How to Start
STEP 01

Start From a Page

Pick the vehicle, capability, or strategy note closest to your field. Read to the open items. The program architecture page maps every domain to the page that carries it.

STEP 02

Name the Item

Write with the page and the item in the first line. "SIDA-F-001, depth of investigation, here is what a small aperture actually resolves" gets a faster and better answer than a general introduction.

STEP 03

Expect a Technical Reply

The first exchange is about the engineering. Agreements, non-disclosure where the software or a partner's detail requires it, and commercial terms follow once both sides know there is something to build.
