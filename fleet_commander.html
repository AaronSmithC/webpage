// 01 Ground Segment Overview
Safety Boundary
Mission-Critical
Not safety-critical. If the coordinator goes down, every vehicle holds its current mode safely under its own autonomous flight software.

Each tanker flies its own safety-critical flight software — autonomous flight, fault detection, and safing all live on the vehicle. The Fleet Commander is the ground-side brain: it never flies a ship, it orchestrates the fleet.

It talks to all 45 vehicles over the same CCSDS-over-UDP link an individual operator console uses — every tanker is an independent command and telemetry endpoint. The coordinator aggregates per-vehicle telemetry into fleet-wide situational awareness at 1 Hz and issues a small, well-defined command vocabulary back to each ship.

// 04 What It Coordinates
01 — DISPATCH

Sortie Scheduling

Selects which vehicle flies the next sortie from state-of-charge, thermal state, maintenance status and position — maximising water delivered per unit time.

02 — DECONFLICT

Pad & Berth Management

Each surface pad is a conveyor: a returning tanker lands on the shortest queue, only its head is serviced and a launch advances the line, so dispatch is limited by vehicle availability and not by pad count. Six station berths sit behind a FIFO berthing queue when the station is saturated.

03 — RESERVE

Standby Allocation

Holds one to two highest-charge vehicles in emergency reserve per the fleet spec — excluded from routine water duty, ready for emergency tasking.

04 — REROUTE

Surface-to-Surface Routing

Suborbital hops between LUNET nodes to reposition the fleet where the next demand is, without a return to station between legs. The duty type exists in the coordinator; routing logic is not yet built.

05 — RESPOND

Anomaly Response

When a vehicle goes SAFE or ABORT, its pending sortie is reassigned and its pad freed — the fleet heals around the loss without operator intervention. A vehicle past its mode's flight-time limit is called stuck and flagged.

06 — OPTIMISE

Throughput & Awareness

Aggregates 45 telemetry streams into fleet-wide situational awareness, paces each ship's simulator through its coasts, and drives the conveyor of sorties that keeps water flowing to station.

// 05 The Fleet, as Run in Simulation

The coordinator exists and runs the fleet closed-loop in simulation, as of October 2026. It is one Python ground process — scheduler, pad and berth manager, tracker, phase driver, anomaly handler, the console's WebSocket server — that spawns 45 instances of the tanker's flight software on one host (a cFS core and a simulated plant each, ninety processes, a load average of about two on sixteen cores), reads their telemetry on 45 UDP ports and broadcasts one fleet state a second. The division of labour is the ship's. Guidance requests its own transitions where physics decides them — COAST at main-engine cutoff, INSERTION at apoapsis, COAST again when circular and when the periapsis is down, DESCENT at the braking gate, CAPTURED_SURFACE at touchdown — and the coordinator sends only the gates that stand on a shared resource or an operation, each when the telemetry says the ship is ready for it: ASCENT once propulsion reports ready, BERTHING once guidance reports orbit, CAPTURED_STATION at the head of the berth FIFO with a berth free, RELEASE after the station swap, SWAP_COMPLETE after the surface turnaround. It paces each simulator through the two 48-minute coasts at 20× and returns it to real time for the insertion and the braking burns, and its stuck detection compares flight seconds in a mode against that mode's limit from the flight profile, not a wall clock.

The run shown is the fleet from a fresh launch to its 86th completion: two full water cycles of the 43 ships in service with the usual two in reserve, recorded tick by tick from the coordinator's own broadcast (3,013 states, 174,754 words) with no label of any kind on the tape — no anomaly, no stuck call, no comms loss, no safing in any vehicle. The dispatcher gave the 43 a sortie in the same tick and sent their ASCENT gates within one second, so the fleet flew as a formation, and the queues are what spread it. All 43 entered BERTHING within one tick and the berth FIFO reached 39 deep; the captures went through six berths, the landings spread over 99 s, and the sortie ends over 203 s as the pad conveyors turned the heads around one by one. The second cycle held that spread at every stage and added nothing to it; the berth queue barely formed. Mean cycle 1,481 s, utilization 0.96. That is the decision taken: no dispatch cadence. The surge is what the system produces and the queues absorb it.

The surge, tick 949: all 43 ships in service entered BERTHING within one tick. Four are at berths and 39 wait in the FIFO under the station; the reserve pair sit above pads 1 and 2. Every element drawn is a field of the coordinator's 1 Hz broadcast. FC-SIM-001
The second cycle, tick 2509: 43 sorties completed, mean cycle 1,481 s, utilization 0.96. The formation has dissolved into a string around the loop — 34 coasting, one inserting, two berthing, two at the station, four deorbiting — and the berth FIFO is two deep. FC-SIM-002

What the coordinator does not do is stated as plainly. It holds no position for anything: pad proximity is a site name, there is no map, and the loop in the picture is the sortie, not geometry. Every simulator has its own ground and no station, so the six conveyors and six berths are the coordinator's bookkeeping laid over 45 private pads until the vehicles share one simulated world, which is the open architecture decision the tanker page names. The simulation is deterministic, and arrivals four seconds apart are served faster than they come, so the queues never grow past the first cycle. The gates still go out as SET_MODE rather than as named verbs: the ship-and-ground boundary of ADR-0005 in everything but vocabulary. No fault is on a fleet tape yet; the anomaly response — a SAFE or ABORT vehicle's sortie reassigned, RESUME alone — and the stuck call are covered by the 97 unit tests, and the abort path and the operator's acts are the next tapes. Surface-to-surface routing is a duty type without routing logic. The fleet has run on one host against simulated vehicles; nothing has run against a vehicle.
