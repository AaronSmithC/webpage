Lunar Water Logistics

Supporting the completion of Aegis Station and launching the first off-Earth water economy.

Baseline: 1g Configuration, Hydrolox Fleet

Aegis Station is baselined at 1g artificial gravity, which sets the shielding requirement at 3.3 million metric tons of lunar water and sizes the tanker fleet and logistics architecture below.

The fleet delivers 2,025 tons per day with 45 tankers for a projected fill time under 5 years. Every tanker flies on LOX/LH₂ split from lunar water at the pad—no fuel is supplied from Earth in any phase. Shielding depth is 3 meters of lunar-sourced water along each ring's outer hull. See the Lunar Tanker Fleet and Lunar Orbital Propellant Depot pages for the vehicle and the first orbital customer.

1. Executive Summary

Aegis Station's three-ring configuration and central hub require over 3,300,000 metric tons of water—equivalent to 1,320 Olympic-sized swimming pools—to complete its radiation shielding and life-support reserves. Delivering that volume from Earth would be prohibitively expensive and logistically unsustainable.

Instead, the station will be filled entirely with lunar-sourced water, mined and lifted from the Moon’s surface. Our logistics baseline calls for a fleet of 45 reusable tankers, each delivering 45 tons of water per trip. This system will fill Aegis Station in approximately 4.5 years and continue operating beyond that point to support:

  • Fuel production (via electrolysis: H₂ + O₂)
  • Orbital depot refueling (cislunar and LEO)
  • Life support reserves for long-duration crews
  • Shielding and utility water for future habitats or spacecraft
The water economy doesn’t end with Aegis—it begins with it.

3. Delivery Architecture

  • Fleet: 45 reusable tankers
  • Payload: 45 tons each
  • Daily Throughput: 2,025 tons/day
  • Fill Time: ~4.5 years
  • Estimated Trips: 73,333
  • Propellant: LOX/LH₂, both produced from lunar water—no Earth-supplied fuel
  • Transfer unit: 45-ton Modular Tank Cartridge, common to surface, tanker, depot and station

The delivery loop. Every sortie carries water up and an empty cartridge down; nothing on the loop is supplied from Earth.

4. Lunar ISRU Operations

Water is extracted from permanently shadowed craters near the lunar south pole using microwave heating and thermal processing. The resulting vapor is condensed, purified, and loaded into standard water cartridges. Water needs no cryogenic handling; the only cryogens in the chain are the propellants split from it at the pad.

  • Excavation and thermal separation
  • Solar/nuclear power sources
  • Autonomous mining and transfer systems

ISRU Process Flow

From ice in the crater to water in a cartridge and hydrolox in the tanker. Water is never a cryogen; the propellants split from it are.

5. Logistics Infrastructure

Surface systems:

  • Launch pads with dust control
  • Cartridge fill and staging
  • Pad-side electrolysis and liquefaction—the refinery that fuels the fleet
  • LUNET utility nodes for surface mobility and inter-node exchange

Orbital systems (at Aegis):

  • Cartridge docking ports and water transfer lines
  • Lunar Orbital Propellant Depot—the first cartridge customer, online before the station
  • Water routing and shield integration
  • Autonomous transfer monitoring

6. Long-Term Applications

  • H₂ + O₂ fuel production
  • LEO and Gateway refueling
  • Life support and redundancy reserves
  • Shielding for future vehicles and platforms
  • Orbital water storage and mission staging

This infrastructure will remain in service long after Aegis is filled—fueling missions, habitats, and platforms across cislunar space.
